## Reading

Second run, on the demo seminars as they are now: Prof Wynter's lines cloned from her own
enrolled sample (the first run, with a synthetic "Prof Lim", is
[`listener-20261107-synthetic-prof.md`](listener-20261107-synthetic-prof.md)).

- **A new failure the first run didn't have.** In 07-guest, pyannote gave the last 2.5 s of the
  professor's final line ("retrieval is usually the better tool. Let's stop there.") to Tina's
  speaker, and Whisper cut that stretch into three segments of 0.96, 0.70 and 0.62 s. The veto
  only checks segments of 1 s or more, so none was checked and W+veto credited a professor
  line to Tina: 1 line, 2.3 s of speech to the wrong student.
- **W + veto + turn (now the app's Listener).** The same voice check also runs on each whole
  turn: consecutive segments of one diarised speaker (`turn_runs`). The three short segments
  are one 2.5 s turn, which scores prof 0.78 against Tina 0.07, so it becomes "ambiguous".
  A segment is credited only if neither its own voice nor its turn's names someone else. No
  line and no speech is credited to the wrong student; all 36 professor lines are right.
- **What's left.** In 06-dominant pyannote found 3 speakers for 5 people, merging Tina's and
  Tasmin's single short lines into other speakers. The veto catches both, so they are
  unattributed, not miscredited: 24/26 student lines right.
- **Why not also rescue the vetoed lines?** A vetoed turn's own voice often names the right
  person (here, the professor), but the app only marks it ambiguous: a wrong credit is the
  error that can change a student's grade, an ambiguous line only costs the professor one
  check in step ② (Who spoke?), where every uncertain line is shown.
- **Thresholds** again make no difference anywhere in the grid (kept at 0.35 / 0.10).
- **Whisper**: 4.3% WER (base, beam 5, word timings), higher than the first run's 3.3%:
  F5-TTS clones of a lecture recording are less clean than macOS `say`. Most errors are names
  and terms ("Laura" for LoRA, "kale penalty" for KL penalty).
- **Runtime** (4-core CPU, nothing else running): 833 s for 498 s of audio (1.7x real time);
  pyannote is 623 s of it (75%). The turn check adds one voice score per turn of several
  segments (30 turns here, about 15 s).

Caveats: as in the first run, 7 scripted recordings with cloned voices, no crosstalk or room
noise, and 26 student lines, so differences are one line each. The guest (macOS `say`) is
now enrolled nowhere: the old "Prof Demo" voice, the same `say` voice, has been removed.

# Week 2: Network effects

## Slide 1
Network effects. Why some products become more valuable as more people use them.

## Slide 2
Economies of scale versus network effects. Economies of scale are a supply-side advantage: the average cost of serving each customer falls as output grows, because fixed costs are spread over more units. Network effects are a demand-side advantage: the product becomes more valuable to each user as more people use it. Both reward size, so they are often confused. The test is where the advantage comes from: lower cost per unit means scale; higher value per user means network effects.

## Slide 3
Direct network effects: users benefit from more users of the same kind, as in a messaging app or a telephone network. Indirect, or cross-side, network effects: one group benefits from more members of another group, as with buyers and sellers on a marketplace, or riders and drivers on a ride-hailing app.

## Slide 4
The chicken-and-egg problem. A two-sided platform is worth little to either side until the other side shows up. Common fixes: subsidise one side, supply one side yourself at first, or start in a narrow niche where both sides are easy to gather.

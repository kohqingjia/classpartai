# Week 4 reading: Setting prices when there are two sides

## Page 1
On a two-sided platform the price charged to one side changes the value the platform offers the other. A low price for riders brings more riders, and more riders make the platform more attractive to drivers, so the two prices cannot be set in isolation.

## Page 2
The main rule is to subsidise the side whose participation creates the larger benefit for the other side and which is more price-sensitive, and to recover the cost from the side that is less price-sensitive. Credit-card networks illustrate this: cardholders often pay nothing or even earn rewards, while merchants pay a fee on every transaction, because merchants value access to cardholders more than cardholders value access to any one merchant.

## Page 3
Subsidies are hard to withdraw. A side that has grown used to free access may leave when prices rise, especially if it can multi-home and switch to a rival with little effort.

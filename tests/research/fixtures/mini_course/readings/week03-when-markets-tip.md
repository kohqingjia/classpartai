# Week 3 reading: When platform markets tip

## Page 1
Network effects pull a market towards concentration. When a platform's value to each user rises with the number of users, an early lead can become self-reinforcing, and the market may tip towards one winner. Tipping is not inevitable, however. Three conditions weaken it.

## Page 2
First, multi-homing. When users can cheaply use several platforms at once, the larger network loses its grip. Ride-hailing is the standard example: riders keep two or three apps on their phones and compare waiting times, and many drivers log into more than one app during a shift. Because both sides multi-home, the network effect is shared rather than captured, and two or more platforms can survive in the same city.

## Page 3
Second, local network effects. A rider in Singapore gains nothing from drivers in Jakarta. When network effects are local, a platform must win city by city, and a strong local rival can hold its ground against a larger regional player. Third, differentiation: when users want different things, such as price, safety features or payment options, a smaller platform can win a segment.

## Page 4
The reading concludes that ride-hailing is better described as a market with a few large players than as a winner-take-all market: network effects are real but local, and multi-homing on both sides limits how much any single platform can capture.
